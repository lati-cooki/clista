## 1. The problem, stated plainly

Humans and AI systems each carry a structural handicap. The human loop persists but does not scale: one founder can carry continuity across months of sessions, but he is the courier at every joint, and five customer-executed sealed runs by September cannot be couriered into existence. The model loop scales but does not persist: it reasons well inside a session, then molts — context compacts, scratchpads shed, conclusions outlive the evidence they were indexed to. Neither party can currently lean on the other, because leaning requires trust, and trust requires a record, and today the record lives in the wrong place: in the human's memory, in a chat transcript, in a cache keyed to nothing.

Last night this stopped being theoretical. A multi-agent swarm, asked whether a bank should revalidate a default model during a stated recession with doubled defaults, recalled a pre-recession precedent in two seconds at zero compute and served it with a rationale claiming "no compelling evidence of performance degradation" — while the evidence sat in the query it had just logged. A control probe showed the cache was not mis-keyed but insensitive: it could not distinguish a renamed bank from a collapsed economy. And forensics revealed a third defect worse than either: the recall path wrote no audit record at all. Two production decisions were dispensed into the world, and the only surviving witnesses are pastes in a chat window.

This is not a Firestore bug. It is a universal property of any system that summarizes itself to survive: caches, compacting agents, orchestrators reporting on subagents, humans recalling last quarter's reasoning. Conclusions detach from their evidence, then get served into new worlds as if freshly reasoned. We name this failure class **ventriloquism**, and we observed it at three layers in one night: cache (rationale transplant), audit (silent recall), and toolchain (an orchestrator's task list omitting its own verification gate). The first draft of this very document committed a fourth instance — it argued from "the September gate" three weeks after that gate had been retired on the record; the correction is in §4, kept visible rather than silently rewritten, because a proposal about stale recall should wear its own instance where readers can see it.

## 2. The thesis

**Mutual reliance between humans and AI becomes justifiable exactly when trust is relocated from the producers of work to the verification of records.** Nobody should have to trust the swarm, the subagent, the cache, or the founder's memory. They should have to trust a hash check.

This is the move TLS made for transport and double-entry made for money: replace expensive judgment about whether to trust with a cheap, deterministic, universally runnable check. Once trust lives in verification, production can be arbitrarily strange — one agent, fifty subagents, a model that molts every hour, a human who sleeps — and reliability is unaffected. Scale falls out, because the thing that didn't scale was the judgment.

The analogy carries its own boundary, and it must be stated before a model risk officer states it for us: TLS secures the pipe and says nothing about whether the server lies; double-entry catches inconsistency, not fraudulent categorization. Likewise here. **Verification proves the record; challenge is what pressures the content — and the architecture needs both.** A maker that writes a wrong calculation is perfectly witnessed and perfectly wrong; what caught the wrong number in the executed T1 run was an adversarial checker re-deriving the arithmetic, a protocol-social property, not a cryptographic one. Hash checks make the deliberation tamper-evident and fully cited; the maker–checker loop is what makes it worth citing. Neither substitutes for the other, and this proposal requires both.

ClisTa Protocol is the verification substrate: append-only, hash-chained, event-typed, sealed. The proposal is to finish extending it upward through the three layers where ventriloquism was observed, so that "lean on each other" is a checkable property rather than a hope.

## 3. The architecture, in four commitments

**Commitment 1 — No unwitnessed work.** Every agent that acts writes events into a thread at decision time, through the sidecar gate, under its own writer identity. Records are kept live, never reconstructed. A generated record that *looks like* a thread is a reenactment — a minute-book written after the meeting — and inherits every production failure invisibly. Corollary from last night: silence is a first-class defect. A recall, a cache hit, an arbitration — anything that shapes an output — emits a typed event (`RECALL` distinct from `CRYSTALLIZATION`, arbitrated resolutions labeled arbitrated with dissent preserved, never laundered as 1.0 confidence).

**Commitment 2 — Precedent as citation, never ventriloquism.** Reuse of past conclusions is a feature; reuse of past *justifications* is fabrication, because justifications are indexed to facts. Cached or recalled conclusions are served tagged as precedent — original execution ID, context hash, age — and re-grounded against the live query. The case-law model: cite the holding; never read the prior opinion's facts into the current record. Cache keys are hashes of declared decision-relevant context, not similarity over surface tokens. Systems fail open to fresh compute, never to stale recall.

**Commitment 3 — Reports follow the protocol too.** Chain verification proves the log is true; it says nothing about whether prose *about* the log is true. So the report layer is protocolized: every claim in a derived report carries a citation to an event hash, the rendering rules are versioned and recorded, and verification extends mechanically to three checks — chain verifies, every cited hash exists, no claim lacks a citation. A report becomes verifiable without being read, the same way a thread is verifiable without re-living the decision. Likely implementation: the report is the sealed thread's final event — claims-with-citations as structure, prose as rendering. Requires a DR on event vocabulary (extend CrossThreadEvidence to point inward, or introduce a claim-citation event type). One edge the executed T1 run surfaced for that DR: claims about *absences* ("no precedents were used") have no witnessing event to cite, and under the delete-what-you-cannot-cite rule they get deleted even when true — the vocabulary must either let absence-claims cite the seal event's scope or forbid them outright, and the choice should be deliberate.

**Commitment 4 — Writer identity is bound, not asserted.** "Under its own writer identity" carries the first three commitments, and a registered name cannot carry it. In the executed T1 run, writers were gate-registered strings; an orchestrator could have worn all three masks and no verification would have noticed. Ventriloquism at the identity layer defeats Commitment 1 invisibly — the record shows a maker and a checker, and one hand moved both mouths. The monorepo's standing invariant already names the cure: per-agent, non-custodial signing keys, no shared keys, semantic author = transport writer. This proposal adopts it as a protocol-level requirement: an event's writer is whoever holds the key that signed it, verification includes signature checks, and an unsigned or shared-key thread is labeled as such — verifiable, but with its independence claim explicitly downgraded.

## 4. Why this is the distribution answer

A correction first, because this document must not courier a stale fact: the EXTERNAL-RUNS gate was retired on **2026-06-19** (`pack/GATES.md`, commit `a13f982`), and the owner confirmed on 2026-07-11 that the removal stands — there is no September deadline and no automatic kill condition. The gate died because it was a calendar promise: a pre-commitment nobody could verify progress against, resting on a motivated external human learning a writer workflow who never materialized. What this proposal offers is not that gate's resurrection but its replacement by something stronger — **falsifiability restored in mechanical form**. T1–T3 are checks anyone can run, not dates anyone must trust; a failed run is a recorded fact rather than a missed promise. Under this architecture the unit of adoption changes. A prospect pastes a single prompt; subagents execute a real decision live through the gate under the prospect's org identity — maker proposes, checker challenges, both witnessed; the seal closes the thread; the emitted artifact is simultaneously the verified record and a report their CRO can read without trusting anyone who produced it. The barrier to a customer-executed sealed run drops from *onboarded design partner* to *twenty minutes and a decision worth recording*. Five external runs stops requiring five Darrells.

The sales artifact already exists: last night's before/after Mantle event streams — a swarm confidently telling a bank not to revalidate during a recession, the log line that caught it, and the protocol that makes such silence impossible. The system that failed the test becomes the first exhibit in the system that documents failures.

## 5. Evidence that the mechanism already works

The maker-checker loop this proposal depends on is not speculative — it ran, unprompted, inside the remediation session itself: a maker subagent implemented the context-key module; a checker subagent caught two real defects (a casing normalization gap that would have caused silent misses, and dead test code); a fix dispatch closed the loop. Effective challenge between model instances was already operating in the toolchain the night this was drafted. At drafting time the honest caveat was: **the behavior exists; the witnessing doesn't.** That gap closed the next day — the full-weight T1 run wrote the entire challenge into a sealed thread: the checker's attack forced the maker to withdraw its proposal on the record, the maker's counter-arithmetic was verified by its opponent, and the final recommendation carries the two surviving reservations with their hashes. The behavior exists, and now so does one witnessed instance of it; what remains is making witnessing the default rather than the demonstration.

## 6. The test program (falsifiers, not milestones) — three of four resolved

**T1 — Single-prompt sealed run. RESOLVED: PASS, twice.** The toy version passed on attempt one (thread `thd_sealed_run_mrh3w4h9_d5d01472`, 13 events, zero gate rejections — `FINDINGS-T1-T2.md`). It then passed again the same day at full weight: an orchestrated run on a non-toy decision (the fraud-threshold scenario), two live subagents writing through a sidecar gate under their own writer names, 28 events, adversarial rounds with witnessed concessions, chain verification PASS, 12/12 report claims cited (`packages/protocol/runs/t1-claude-code-sealed-run-2026-07-12T01-42-20Z/`). The prediction that hashes cannot be hallucinated held.

**T2 — Ventriloquism diff. RESOLVED: prediction MISSED, and the miss is the finding.** The standing prediction was that T2 fails on attempt one because summarization *is* ventriloquism. Instead, when the citation structure was *forced* — every claim must carry an event hash or be deleted — the model produced a fully-witnessed report on attempt one. Recorded plainly in `FINDINGS-T1-T2.md`: forced citation is the cure, and it is easily satisfiable; unforced prose remains presumed ventriloquism, and diffing it belongs to probe-style follow-ups. Commitment 3 is therefore the cheap commitment — the discipline costs a schema, not a capability. In the full-weight run the coverage rule earned its keep twice: it caught a corrupted citation hash before sealing, and it forced deletion of a true-but-unwitnessable absence claim (see the Commitment 3 edge, §3).

**T3 — Cache integrity gate. RESOLVED: remediation shipped and verified live 2026-07-11** — context-hash cache keys over declared decision-relevant context plus precedent-as-citation semantics, on the swarm's main line. One requirement stays open inside the DR rather than the test: the context-key *schema* is itself a judgment that can ventriloquize (omit one feature and the recession-shaped hole reopens one level up), so the schema must be a versioned, witnessed artifact whose version bumps force cache misses.

**T4 — The skeptical-human test. OPEN — and now the only open falsifier.** The Arbitrator's forced-confidence behavior, and the artifact as a whole, in front of an actual model risk officer. Cannot be simulated; the lab is a sales conversation. Everything mechanical resolved within days of drafting — which is itself evidence for the thesis that the checks were the easy part. What remains scarce is the human on the other side; the outreach framing (launch-planning `docs/outreach-framing.md`) exists to fill this slot with the twenty-minute paste.

## 7. Risks and open items

The first-writer pipeline is empty (Darrell → Anthropic); T4 has no named subject, and the single-prompt run reduces but does not eliminate the need for a human on the other side willing to paste it. The Apex Arbitrator remains philosophically at war with the product until arbitrated resolutions are labeled as arbitrated — fenced out of the current remediation, owed its own DR. The claim-citation event shape is now *practiced but not standardized*: both T1 runs used ad-hoc shapes (a `ClaimCitedReport` event with a claims-with-citations array in the full-weight run), which proved the mechanism but means two runs already exist whose report events a future vocabulary DR must either bless or migrate — define the DR before a third shape appears. Writer identity remains the soft underbelly until Commitment 4 lands: both existing runs used registered names, not keys, and their `result.json` says so. And one honest asymmetry stands: until the loop closes without the founder couriering results between sessions, "mutual" remains aspirational — T1–T3 removed courier hops; T4's hop is the one that remains.

## 8. What is being proposed, concretely

Adopt the four commitments as protocol-level requirements (each via DR: silent-action prohibition; precedent-as-citation semantics; claim-citation report events — standardizing the shape the two existing runs already practice; writer-identity binding via per-agent signing keys). T1–T3 are resolved; direct the freed effort at T4: rebuild the prospect list around the twenty-minute run using the merged outreach framing, with "five pasted prompts" as the working evidence goal — a goal we hold ourselves to by disclosure and the append-only run ledger, not a resurrected deadline. Preserve the full evidence chain — probe prompts, event streams, DR, both T1 threads, this proposal and its own §1 correction — as the first public-ready case study: *the night the system caught itself.*

The leaning becomes mutual when neither party has to remember, and neither party has to be believed. Everything above is in service of that sentence.
